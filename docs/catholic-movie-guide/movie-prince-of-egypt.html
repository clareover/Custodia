A-II · Adults and Adolescents

The Prince of Egypt

1998 · Animated / Biblical · 1h 39m · MPAA: PG

Catholic Summary

A visually stunning and largely reverent retelling of the Exodus story. The film treats God as the true actor in history, shows the horror of oppression, and presents Moses’ call and the plagues with real weight. While it takes some artistic liberties with the biblical text, it remains one of the most respectful and powerful mainstream depictions of a major Old Testament event. Widely praised by many Catholic and Christian reviewers for its beauty and seriousness.

Content Details & Timestamps

The main concerns are intense biblical violence and thematic weight rather than anything gratuitous or impure.

Violence / Intense Sequences

  • Opening Egyptian soldiers seize Hebrew infant boys. The act itself is mostly off-screen or shown through hieroglyphic imagery; still emotionally heavy.
  • ~0:25 Moses accidentally causes the death of an Egyptian overseer while trying to stop the whipping of a slave. The fall is shown; the death is clear but not graphic.
  • Plagues sequence The ten plagues are depicted with intensity (Nile turns to blood, boils, locusts, fire from the sky, etc.). Not gory, but the scale and suffering are felt.
  • ~1:15 The Angel of Death / death of the firstborn. A shadowy, ethereal presence moves through homes. We hear gasps and see limited visual cues (a child’s arm going limp). Emotionally powerful and the heaviest scene in the film.
  • Climax The parting of the Red Sea and the destruction of Pharaoh’s army. Dramatic and large-scale; the drowning is not lingered on in a gruesome way.

Other Notes

  • Various Male characters often wear only loincloths (period-appropriate, not sexualized). Brief mild romantic tension between Moses and Tzipporah; one chaste kiss.

Language

None of concern.

Worldview & Moral Themes

Strongly theocentric. God is portrayed as the true deliverer; Moses is a reluctant instrument. The film condemns the oppression of the Hebrews and shows the hardening of Pharaoh’s heart with moral seriousness. Some artistic changes from the biblical text exist (e.g., the exact circumstances of the Egyptian’s death, certain character motivations), but the core truth of divine intervention, justice, and liberation is retained and treated with respect.

Positive / Redeeming Elements

  • God is clearly the hero of the story
  • Powerful depiction of divine power and the cost of hardening one’s heart against God
  • Themes of vocation, courage, and solidarity with the oppressed
  • Stunning animation and a score that elevates the sacred drama
  • Useful introduction to the Exodus narrative for older children and teens when watched with guidance

Family Guidance

Best for ages 8–10 and up, or younger children with a parent present who can pause and explain. The opening infant-killing sequence, the accidental death, the plagues, and especially the death of the firstborn are the scenes most likely to disturb sensitive viewers. These are biblical events treated with weight rather than exploitation, but they are not light. Excellent for family discussion about God’s justice, mercy, and the meaning of the Passover / Exodus as preparation for Christ.
