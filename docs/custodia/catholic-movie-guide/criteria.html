Our Catholic Criteria

How we evaluate films and form our guidance

This guide seeks to help Catholic families and individuals watch media with a well-formed conscience. Our approach is rooted in the moral teaching of the Catholic Church as expressed in Sacred Scripture, the Catechism of the Catholic Church, and the perennial wisdom of the saints and magisterium.

Overall Rating Scale

We use a scale inspired by the former classifications of the U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops’ Office for Film and Broadcasting (and the earlier Legion of Decency tradition), adapted for clarity:

A-I
General Patronage
Suitable for all ages. Content is wholesome or presents no significant moral difficulty for children or adults.
A-II
Adults and Adolescents
Appropriate for older teens and adults. May contain some mature themes or mild content that younger children should avoid.
A-III
Adults
Suitable for mature adults. Contains more significant content concerns (language, violence, thematic material) that require adult discernment.
L
Limited Adult Audience
Problematic content that many adults will find troubling. Requires careful judgment; may still have artistic or thematic value for well-formed viewers.
O
Morally Offensive
Content that is gravely contrary to Catholic moral teaching (e.g., the celebration of abortion, blasphemy treated lightly, explicit endorsement of gravely immoral acts). Generally to be avoided.

What We Examine

Beyond counting “bad words” or scenes of violence, we consider:

  • Language — Profanity, vulgarity, and especially blasphemy or irreverent use of the holy name of God or sacred things.
  • Sexual content & nudity — Depictions or implications of sexual activity outside of marriage, objectification, and the treatment of the human body and sexuality in light of Catholic teaching on chastity and the dignity of the person.
  • Violence — Graphicness, sadism, glorification of unjust violence, or the trivialization of human life.
  • Treatment of the sacred — How God, the Church, the sacraments, the saints, prayer, and religious life are portrayed (reverent, neutral, or hostile/mocking).
  • Life issues — Abortion, euthanasia, suicide, and the broader culture of life or death.
  • Occult & New Age — Depictions of magic, witchcraft, demonic activity, or alternative spiritualities presented without proper Catholic context or warning.
  • Worldview & moral message — Does the film ultimately affirm truth, goodness, and beauty consistent with the Catholic faith, or does it promote moral relativism, the rejection of natural law, or hostility to the Church?

Positive Elements

We also note genuine artistic merit, moments of virtue, depictions of sacrifice, forgiveness, the beauty of creation, authentic family life, and any explicit or implicit Christian insight. A film is not judged solely by what it avoids, but also by what it offers.

Important Note

These ratings and analyses are offered as guidance to assist the faithful in forming their own judgments. They are not binding on conscience. Parents remain the primary educators of their children and the best judges of what is suitable for their particular family.
